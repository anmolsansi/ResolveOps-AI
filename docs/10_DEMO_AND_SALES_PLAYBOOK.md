# ResolveOps AI — Demo and Sales Playbook

## Purpose

This document explains how to demonstrate and sell ResolveOps under the project’s **complete-before-charge** policy.

> **Do not sell a package while part of the promised package is unfinished.**

Sales should show a clearly defined completed product, explain exactly what is included and excluded, and make claims that match the release the client will receive.

# 1. First commercial package

The recommended first completed package is **ResolveOps AI Support Intelligence and Agent Assistance**.

Its plain-English promise is:

> ResolveOps helps a support team reuse approved historical support knowledge, find relevant previous solutions, draft answers with supporting evidence, identify questions it cannot safely answer, preserve customer context, escalate uncertain situations to people, and give managers visibility into quality.

---

# 2. Sales discovery

Before the demo, learn whether the prospect has the problem ResolveOps solves.

Ask:

- How large is the support team?
- How many support conversations arrive each month?
- Which questions repeat most often?
- Where do agents look for answers today?
- How much useful knowledge is hidden in old tickets?
- How often do junior agents need senior help?
- How is answer quality measured?
- What happens when an agent is uncertain?
- Which support platform is used?
- Can historical support cases be exported?
- Which information requires special handling?

The purpose is to establish product fit, not to collect a wish list that silently expands the package.

---

# 3. Five-minute demo

## Step 1 — Show approved support knowledge

Explain that ResolveOps works from the company’s support information rather than behaving like an empty generic chatbot.

## Step 2 — Ask a question with a known answer

Show the answer and supporting citations.

A citation tells the support user where the information came from.

## Step 3 — Ask an unsupported question

Show the safe fallback or escalation behavior.

Knowing when not to answer is part of the product.

## Step 4 — Show customer context and human handoff

Demonstrate how conversation history is preserved when a human needs to take over.

## Step 5 — Show quality and manager visibility

Show reliability and analytics information relevant to the completed package.

---

# 4. Included and excluded capabilities

Every proposal and demonstration should clearly distinguish **included** capabilities from **excluded** future capabilities.

An excluded feature is not partial delivery if it was never part of the package promise.

Examples of capabilities that may remain outside the first package include live helpdesk synchronization, voice, messaging channels, high-impact automated actions, enterprise identity features, and later roadmap work.

If a prospect requires an excluded capability in order to use ResolveOps, either:

1. complete that capability before offering the revised package; or
2. state that the current package does not meet that requirement yet.

Do not promise the feature, deliver an incomplete version, and use a reduced amount as the solution.

---

# 5. Sales claims must match reality

Do not describe:

- a mock integration as a live integration;
- connector architecture as a completed vendor connector;
- a human-reviewed action as fully autonomous;
- a planned channel as supported;
- unverified production behavior as production-proven;
- an AI answer system as incapable of mistakes.

A strong sales message is accurate enough that the engineering team would be comfortable hearing it repeated to the client.

---

# 6. Common objections

## “Why not use a general AI chat tool?”

ResolveOps adds the support workflow around the AI: approved company knowledge, evidence/citations, quality tracking, failed-query review, customer context, human handoff, workspace separation, and operational controls.

## “Can the AI be wrong?”

Yes. ResolveOps is designed to reduce and expose that risk through retrieval, citations, safe fallback, human involvement, and quality measurement. Do not claim that AI errors are impossible.

## “Can it replace our support agents?”

The first package is designed to make support work faster and more consistent. More automation should be offered only when the relevant production integrations and safety controls are complete.

## “Do you support our helpdesk?”

Answer according to the completed package. If a real connector is not included, say so clearly.

## “Can it perform high-impact actions automatically?”

Only claim actions that have completed integrations, permissions, safety controls, auditability, failure handling, and any required approval process.

---

# 7. Proposal structure

A client proposal should contain:

- the business problem;
- exact package name;
- exact included capabilities;
- exact excluded capabilities;
- data requirements;
- deployment approach;
- security responsibilities;
- support and incident process;
- success measures;
- commercial terms.

The proposal should not depend on silent promises that core product features will be built after the client starts using it.

---

# 8. Configuration versus incomplete product work

Client onboarding naturally includes configuration such as:

- creating a workspace;
- adding users;
- importing approved data;
- configuring supported settings;
- setting thresholds and retention rules.

That is normal onboarding.

By contrast, building a promised core feature after the package has been accepted is incomplete product delivery.

The completed package should already contain the product capabilities that were advertised.

---

# 9. New client requests

When a prospect requests a new capability, classify it.

### Required for the promised outcome

Add it to the required scope and finish it before offering that revised package.

### Useful but optional

Keep it outside the current package.

### Repeated strategic request

Plan it for the next standard ResolveOps package and finish that version before advertising it as included.

This prevents the product from becoming an endless collection of unfinished custom promises.

---

# 10. Complete-package commercial model

ResolveOps should use package definitions such as:

- **Package A — Support Intelligence and Agent Assistance**;
- **Package B — Connected Support Operations** with completed live support-platform integrations;
- **Package C — Controlled Action Automation** with completed permissions and approval controls;
- **Package D — Omnichannel Support** with completed messaging or voice channels.

Each package can provide more value than the previous one, but every package should be complete inside its own promise when offered.

---

# 11. First-client onboarding

The client should receive the same product behavior that was demonstrated.

Onboarding may include:

1. workspace creation;
2. user configuration;
3. approved data import;
4. production AI configuration;
5. client-specific evaluation questions;
6. permission verification;
7. support workflow walkthrough;
8. manager dashboard walkthrough;
9. known limitations and exclusions;
10. support and incident contacts.

The onboarding process should configure a completed product, not finish building its promised core capabilities.

---

# 12. Sales integrity checklist

Before every sales meeting, confirm:

- I am showing a completed release.
- Every claim matches actual behavior.
- Real integrations are distinguished from mocks.
- AI uncertainty is explained accurately.
- Included and excluded capabilities are clear.
- Regulated-industry claims are not made without separate proof.
- The commercial offer corresponds to a complete package.
- I am not promising unfinished work as though it already exists.

---

# 13. Final sales principle

> **What we show is what the client receives. What we list as included is complete. What is future work is clearly identified as future work.**

The sales process should create confidence in a finished product, not negotiate around missing promised functionality.
